# WatchGPT behavior and boundaries

## Reminders and agenda

Every item has a UUID, title, notes, wall-clock date/time, zone policy, recurrence, completion/deletion state, snooze time, alert lead (events only) and a selected alert device. Both devices have the same native editor. A save goes to local encrypted SQLite first and then queues a sync. No Google account or internet is needed for local use. Lists show each item by its next occurrence, in the device's local time and its 12- or 24-hour setting; all-day items stay on their date.

Daily, weekdays, weekly, and monthly repeats are supported. Monthly day 29–31 moves to the last day of shorter months and goes back to the original day in later months; changing only the time of day keeps that day. Completing a repeated item moves it to its next occurrence; deleting it removes the whole series. A weekdays series written for a weekend starts on its first weekday. DST gaps move forward through the gap, and the series keeps its wall-clock time afterwards; overlaps use the first instance of that wall time, avoiding a second alert. “Keep local time when travelling” follows each device's current zone. Turn it off to keep the saved zone; that zone is kept through storage and sync.

### When alerts ring

- An item rings at its time, minus its lead (“Alert minutes before”, for events). All-day items are dates: they alert at 09:00 on their day wherever the device is, and are overdue only once that day is over. An event is overdue once it has ended.
- Snooze postpones the alert that is due (or else the next one) by its minutes. It never brings an alert forward, and it replaces the alerts it postponed: snoozing ahead of an occurrence, or for longer than a day, doesn't also ring the occurrences it covers. A snooze is an absolute instant. The reminders screen offers Snooze only while an alert is due; the notification always offers **Snooze 10 min**.
- **Done** on a notification completes the occurrence it announced, also for an advance alert (Done at 09:45 for a 10:00 item completes 10:00). **Done** on a card completes the occurrence the card shows, or the one that went by in the last half day if Done comes late, or the snoozed one. **Snooze 10 min** on a notification counts from the tap. Done and Snooze on a notification that is no longer current do nothing but clear it: the series has moved past that occurrence (on the other device or by an earlier tap), the one-off was moved or completed, or that snooze was replaced or cleared. A notification also goes by itself once the other device has moved the item on.
- Missed alerts (device off, app restarting, Doze) are still delivered late within a window measured from the time they are for, not from their advance warning: 12 hours for a one-off, 1 hour for an occurrence of a series. So a day-ahead alert missed overnight still rings before the event, and the nearest occurrence of a series keeps its missed advance alert (only that one). Older ones are skipped: a series goes on at its next occurrence, and a new or reset device doesn't ring old history.
- Each device keeps a delivery receipt per occurrence, named by the occurrence's wall-clock time (or the snooze time). An edit that keeps the time, a sync or a time-zone change therefore doesn't ring an alert again; neither does changing only the lead after the alert rang (one alert per occurrence). Receipts from earlier versions are recognised by item and time. Receipts survive restarts.
- When an alarm goes off, the item is checked again. If it was edited elsewhere meanwhile, the alarm is set for the new time instead of ringing or being dropped.
- Notifications use the high-priority “Reminders” channel and are `localOnly`, so the phone's notification isn't mirrored to the watch as an extra alert. They are marked private with a public version that has no content: on a lock screen that hides sensitive notifications (an Android setting), an alert shows only “WatchGPT reminder · Unlock to see it”. Tapping one opens the reminders. Done and Snooze always clear their notification.

### Which device alerts

Alarms are local to the chosen alert device: **Watch**, **Phone**, or **Both**. An item made by hand defaults to the device it is made on. An item made by voice (`add_reminder`, `add_calendar_event`) defaults to **Both** unless the user names one. Upstream's voice reminders and events were phone calendar entries with calendar alerts, which rang on the phone and were mirrored to the watch; and a watch that is off the wrist or charging mustn't swallow a reminder. Completing an item on either device completes it on both once they sync. Watch-targeted items created on the phone must sync before the watch can alert. There is deliberately no automatic phone failover while disconnected: the phone can't know whether the watch already alerted.

### Timing limits

Exact alarms come from `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, which Android grants at install from Android 13. (`SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` is denied by default on Android 14+ and Wear OS 5+.) An Android 12 phone uses `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`, declared only up to API 32; if the user turns that access off, WatchGPT uses an inexact alarm and the reminders screen says “Precise timing is off”. Android can still delay or silence alerts under power saving, Do Not Disturb, disabled notification channels, missing notification permission, or force-stop; exact alarms are not an absolute delivery guarantee. Scheduling resumes after boot (once the device is unlocked), time and zone changes, app update, foreground use, and the periodic background sync (WorkManager, at most every 15 minutes). Android does not let ordinary apps restart after a user force-stop until they are reopened.

### Editing safely

The editor keeps the version it opened. If the other device or the calendar changes the item meanwhile, the editor says so (“Changed on your watch while you were editing…”) and offers **Reload**; if the item was deleted, it says so and offers **Close**. Storage also refuses to save over a version it no longer holds, so nothing is silently overwritten. A double tap on Save can't create two items. Saving a completed item reopens it.

## Device synchronization

Wear Data Layer only connects apps with the same application ID and signing key. Each item is a persistent DataItem that holds the item as JSON, in the same encoding as storage. A device-specific Lamport counter orders revisions without trusting wall-clock agreement. Equal revisions are decided by the author's device ID, then by the canonical encoding, the same way on both devices. A deletion always wins over an offline edit, even one with a higher counter. Deletion records are kept, because a device can be offline for any length of time; they keep only the item's ID, revision and author device, none of its content or schedule. Conflicting edits resolve per whole item; the losing edit is not combined field by field. An open editor can't overwrite a change it hasn't seen (see above).

A sync first takes in the other device's items, then (on the phone) runs the Google Calendar pass, then sends this device's changed items. The calendar pass runs even when no watch is paired or reachable. A record from the other device that can't be read (for example from a newer app version) is skipped instead of blocking sync in both directions; the status counts it: “N item(s) from your watch could not be read; update both apps”. Unchanged items aren't sent again, and sync requests made while one is queued or running are folded into it.

“Sent to Wear Data Layer” means accepted by the local transport with a connected peer, not an end-to-end receipt from the other app. “Saved locally · waiting for your watch” means no peer is connected, and “sync pending” shows an error. Open both apps and use **Sync now** when diagnosing. The periodic background sync (WorkManager, with retries) and the Data Layer listener recover. Bluetooth pairing and Google Play services are required; a disconnected LTE watch can still manage its own reminders but does not independently connect to ChatGPT.

### Where the data is

Local storage is `organizer.db` in the app's no-backup storage, on each device. Each item is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under an Android Keystore key, with its UUID bound as authenticated data. Application backups are disabled. Not encrypted: each row's ID and revision, the delivery receipts (item ID and the time of the alert that rang), and the calendar links (event ID, revision and a hash of the copy). A row that can't be decrypted no longer hides the others; the status counts it. If the Keystore key is gone for good, the old rows are set aside and the device starts over; on the next sync the other device, and this device's own Data Layer copies, bring the items back.

The synced copies are stored by Google Play services on both devices, outside WatchGPT's encryption: every item as plain JSON, including deletion records (which keep no content). WatchGPT never removes them; a deleted item's copy is replaced by its deletion record. Wear sync uses Google's authenticated transport, which can relay through Google's servers. Google Calendar copies, when enabled, live under Google's account and calendar policies. Sign-in and other memory protections remain upstream's.

**Delete everything** (phone Settings) replaces every item with a deletion record, removes the calendar copies at once from every calendar WatchGPT wrote to (also with sync turned off; this needs calendar permission, and Settings says so if it's missing), and clears the calendar opt-in and links. If something can't be removed, Settings says what is left; running it again finishes the job. The watch deletes its items when it next syncs; reconnect it to finish. Uninstalling both apps is different: it removes their local keys and data, but leaves Google calendar copies in place.

## Google Calendar

Google sync is opt-in and phone-only. Only visible Google calendars you own are offered; one that someone else shared with you isn't, since its owner would see every reminder. (A shared calendar chosen with an earlier version keeps working, and the calendar status says to choose one of your own.) WatchGPT writes copies as private events with a `[WatchGPT:UUID]` marker and a “Managed by WatchGPT” line in the description. Reminders are marked free, events busy. Copies have no Calendar reminders: WatchGPT does the alerting, so you aren't alerted twice. WatchGPT only edits or deletes events that carry its marker: a linked copy by its event ID while it still has the item's marker, or a copy found by its marker in the chosen calendar. An event ID alone is never trusted (after Calendar Storage is cleared, IDs can name other events): the copy is found again by its marker, and if it has none, the event is left alone and a new copy is written. Single-occurrence exceptions are never linked. A stable link plus the marker lookup recover an interrupted insert without inserting a second copy.

Each item syncs on its own, so one problem item doesn't hold back the rest. Title, notes, date/time, duration, time zone and repeat edits made in Calendar update WatchGPT and then the watch. Copies are compared by meaning, so Google's sync rewriting `DURATION` (for example as `P1800S`) or `RRULE` (adding `BYDAY` or `WKST`) is not taken for an edit. A calendar edit changes only what a calendar can express; WatchGPT's own settings stay: alert device, alert lead, “keep local time when travelling”, and a snooze while the time is unchanged. An all-day event is read as a date, so it stays on its day instead of moving to UTC midnight. Notes keep text added around the marker, and overlong titles are trimmed. Removing the marker in Google disowns the copy: WatchGPT leaves that event alone from then on and writes a new copy (the calendar status counts these). Simultaneous WatchGPT and Calendar edits keep the WatchGPT version, with a conflict count in the calendar status.

Some calendar changes pause only that item, with a count in the calendar status:

- A copy moved to another calendar. No event outside the chosen calendar is touched; move it back to resume.
- A repeat WatchGPT can't represent exactly: custom intervals, `COUNT` or `UNTIL` end rules, yearly rules, or a monthly rule on a single day 29–31 (a calendar skips shorter months, while WatchGPT's month-end repeat moves to their last day). The item keeps its WatchGPT version, the calendar's rule is not overwritten, and nothing is approximated. Simplify the repeat in Calendar to resume.

Changes made in Calendar to a single occurrence of a series are not followed; WatchGPT keeps the series as it is.

Deleting a linked copy in Calendar deletes the WatchGPT item (for a repeat, the whole series). If several copies vanish at once and none of WatchGPT's are left in the calendar, that is taken for Calendar Storage being cleared, not deletions: no item is deleted, and sync waits for the copies to download again (up to a day) before writing new ones. Deleting an item in WatchGPT deletes its copy. Completing a one-off removes its copy; completing a repeat moves the copy to the next occurrence. Turning sync off leaves existing copies where they are (the calendar screen says so). Choosing another calendar moves the copies: the next sync removes them from the old calendar and writes them to the new one. Copies written by an earlier version are rewritten once as private and free. Turning sync back on for a calendar finds its copies again by their marker, and never deletes items whose copies were removed meanwhile: those get new copies.

“Written to Android Calendar” confirms only the local provider write, not Google server synchronization, which Android's Google account sync does separately. Device and calendar progress are shown separately. The calendar pass runs with each sync: after changes, periodically in the background, when WatchGPT opens, and on Sync now. It is not an instant push subscription. One-off and all-day events are supported; recurring all-day copies use day-based durations. A monthly repeat on day 29–31 is written so that it moves to the last day of shorter months (for the 31st: `BYMONTHDAY=28,29,30,31;BYSETPOS=-1`).

## Voice and native alarms

WatchGPT keeps upstream Buddy's ChatGPT/Codex account OAuth and its undocumented backend realtime endpoint (`/backend-api/codex/realtime/calls`, `intent=quicksilver`, `architecture=avas`). This is not a documented public ChatGPT subscription voice SDK. Official Sign in with ChatGPT offerings do not establish support for this particular endpoint. OpenAI can change the protocol, limits or eligibility without notice. No API key, API billing fallback, new core subscription or hosted database is introduced. Real access requires the owner's interactive sign-in and a real voice test.

Audio interruption, local memory, conversations, tools and upstream privacy gates are retained. Voice reminders and events are WatchGPT items, not phone calendar entries:

- `add_reminder` and `add_calendar_event` save WatchGPT items that alert on both devices unless the user names one. A date-only event is all day and alerts at 09:00.
- `list_reminders` gives the open reminders, soonest first, at the times people say (at most 15 named), without IDs.
- `list_calendar_events` reads up to 31 days of agenda: WatchGPT's own events and reminders, which need no calendar access, plus the phone's calendar when calendar access is on. WatchGPT's calendar copies are listed once. At most 15 entries.
- Both list tools read private data. With “Private things stay on this phone” on (the default), the phone answers from them itself and ChatGPT doesn't get them.
- `change_reminder` completes, snoozes, edits or deletes an item by the exact title the user said (case is ignored). Two items with that title are refused, never guessed. A new time or repeat reopens a completed item; a change of time of day keeps a month-end series' day; an unreadable new time is refused. If the item changed on the other device at that moment, nothing is saved and ChatGPT is told so.
- Deleting can't be undone and reaches the watch and the calendar, so a voice delete is read back and waits for the user's yes in a later turn, the same way texts and calls do (`Pending`, `confirm_action`): within two minutes, with the user present, counted in the hourly limit.

Native Clock intents support repeated weekdays when the installed Clock app honors `EXTRA_DAYS`. Android offers no general API to read a third-party Clock app's alarms, so results say only what happened. “ok:” means the request was sent to Clock with WatchGPT on screen; there is no receipt, and the user can check in Clock. “not done yet:” means WatchGPT was in the background: it posted a notification that the user must tap within 15 minutes and confirm in Clock (with notifications off, it asks the user to open WatchGPT instead). “error:” means nothing was sent. A “not done yet” is logged as waiting, and the phone's pop-up card appears only for “ok”.

A call ends after the conversation timeout of silence: 30 seconds by default, 10 seconds to 15 minutes, or “Until I stop”, which still ends after 30 minutes of silence. Before anyone has spoken, a call ends within a minute whatever the setting, so an accidental wake-up doesn't hold the microphone and a ChatGPT call open. An error event from the voice service in the middle of a call is logged and the call goes on; if the service ends the call, that is shown.

The watch remains a companion with a tile, launcher/double-press activation (and Knock knock, which opens an app), an `ACTION_ASSIST` entry for the Home button's press and hold once the user makes WatchGPT the watch's digital assistant, and local wake recognition. During a conversation the bezel sets the answer volume (the call stream, which plays the answers so the watch can cancel their echo). With the phone's **Play answers as media** on, the phone tells the watch at the start of each call (`route`'s `media`): answers play as media and the bezel sets the media volume, and with no echo cancelling the watch's mic sends nothing while an answer plays and for 350 ms after (the listening chime excepted). A tap on the face while an answer plays as media stops it on the watch and opens the mic at once, instead of ending the conversation: the rest of that answer is dropped until the phone reports a phase other than speaking, and what the user says next reaches ChatGPT, which stops the old answer as it does when talked over. On the phone, "WatchGPT Talk" (an alias of the main activity) and `VOICE_COMMAND` open it listening, as `ACTION_ASSIST` does. The phone's screen tools refuse with a clear reason while the phone is locked or its screen is off. For “Hey WatchGPT”, **Learn my voice** keeps only spellings of three to five words, so a take heard as just “Hey watch” can't make it wake on everyday speech. **Change phrase** replaces “Hey WatchGPT” with the user's own two to five words (letters said one by one count one each): the watch spells them in the wake model's pieces with the model's own sentencepiece file (two or three capitals such as “GPT”, or a word without a vowel, three ways, as the default is; “Dr” and the like in full). It refuses digits, letters outside English, a spelling of fewer than five or more than twenty pieces, and a phrase made only of everyday words. The engine always listens for keywords.txt too, so with the user's own phrase a “Hey WatchGPT” is heard and ignored. A different phrase drops what Learn my voice learned (the watch asks first when there is something to drop); the same phrase written another way keeps it. Learn my voice then learns the user's phrase: spellings at most as long as the phrase in words, and at least two words, or two fewer, or as few as its run-together spelling. On the phone, opening WatchGPT as the assistant (`ACTION_ASSIST`) starts listening; whether a phone offers WatchGPT as its assistant varies. It is not advertised as replacing Samsung's system assistant. No live-voice or Watch9 hardware behavior is considered verified just because an APK compiles.
